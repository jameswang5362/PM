Project Management

Every Yomura project is planned by the six Ms and reviewed every week on a single page. You see the plan, what happened, and why, for every task from kickoff to launch.

Your drawing, price and launch date become a plan

A new part is more than a mold and a molding machine. It needs a resin that arrives on time, a gauge that has been qualified, an operator who has been trained, and a room that holds the right temperature. Projects slip when one of these is forgotten.

Yomura runs every project from one document, the 6M Weekly Presentation Report. The project manager builds it at kickoff, the team reviews it every week, and it stays open for you to read.

How a project runs

  1. Kickoff

    We confirm scope, launch date and price in a project approval sheet, name the team, and fix a weekly review slot with you.

  2. Plan by the six Ms

    The project manager walks through each M and asks what the job needs. Every answer becomes a task with an owner and a due date.

  3. Review every week

    The team goes through the one-page report. Open tasks come first. Any change is entered with its date and its reason.

  4. Close and learn

    Finished tasks stay on the page. Customer feedback and defects return to the next project through 8D and corrective action requests (CAR).

Planning by the six Ms, not by department

Most project plans follow phases: design, tooling, trial, production. Items that belong to no single phase tend to fall through. We lay out the plan under six headings instead, so each of those items has a place in the table, and an empty place gets noticed.

Man Human Resources

Teamwork, communication, on-the-job training, certification, shift handover. Who approves, who is responsible, who needs to be trained.

Machine Machinery & Equipment

Machines, molds and fixtures, assembly tools, peripheral equipment, material feeding, capacity, preventive maintenance.

Material Material Supply

Chemical and physical properties, RoHS, BOM and MRP, supply chain, storage and logistics. Which resin, from whom, and by when.

Method Method Efficiency

Parameter optimization, error-proofing, SOP, standard time, ergonomics, automation, MES control.

Measure Quality Measurement

Gauge qualification, inspection instruments, SPC, SIP, measurement system analysis (MSA), reliability tests.

Milieu Work Environment

Temperature, humidity, lighting, noise, environmental protection, safety, 5S.

The 6M Weekly Presentation Report

The report is one table. The header gives the week number, project name, issue date, team leader and team members. Six colored markers across the top remind the team that all six Ms must be covered. Below it, each row is one task, filed under one M.

Task and 6M

What has to be done, and which M it belongs to.

Responsible

One named owner for every row.

Initial Due Date

Written once when the project is planned. Never changed.

Actual Due Date

The real completion date, or the adjusted date if a task will run late.

Open / Closed

Where the task stands today. Closed rows stay on the page.

Action Records

The dated reason behind every change, including decisions made by the customer.

We never erase a date

The Initial Due Date is written once and never overwritten. When a task finishes late, or will, the new date goes in the Actual Due Date column and the reason goes in Action Records, with the date of the decision. If a task is reworked, it gets a new row instead of replacing the old one.

Meeting minutes

Complete, but spread over many files. Nobody rereads them to see how one task changed.

A schedule with overwritten dates

Easy to read, but it has no memory. A project that slipped three times looks the same as one that never slipped.

Yomura's control table

Plan, result and reason sit on one line. Anyone who opens the report sees the full history of every task.

Why it matters to you. When you ask why a tool is a week late, we answer from the page, and the page shows the dates of your own decisions as well as ours. The gap between the two date columns also shows where time was lost, so the next project is planned with that lesson built in.

What you can expect from us

  • A named project manager and a fixed weekly review.
  • One page that shows every task, owner and due date under the six Ms.
  • Early warning. A slipping task shows up in the report with its reason, not at the end of the project.
  • Your decisions on record. When you change a requirement, we log it with the date and update the plan.
  • Continuous improvement. Defects and complaints are followed through 8D and CAR and feed the next project.
